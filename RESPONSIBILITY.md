# Responsibility Boundaries

## Purpose

This document protects the project against **responsibility laundering**: the loss, reassignment or silent dilution of responsibility when work is copied, forked, merged, delegated to AI, republished or implemented elsewhere.

It is a repository-governance document, not a legal judgment.

## Responsibility requires an attributable event

For this repository, responsibility should be attached to explicit recorded actions rather than inferred from possession of content, proximity to a decision, or use of a tool.

Examples of attributable events include:

- proposing a change;
- submitting or recording evidence;
- reviewing a proposal;
- requesting changes;
- approving or merging a pull request;
- designating a release, checkpoint or successor;
- publishing an implementation or downstream adaptation;
- explicitly resolving a recorded conflict.

`responsibility requires explicit attributable action`

## Contribution responsibility

A contributor is responsible for the content and representations of the contribution they submit within the scope they actually control.

Acceptance of a contribution does not retroactively make the repository editor its sole author, and does not erase the contributor's recorded provenance.

`merge authority != authorship of every contribution`

## Review responsibility

A reviewer is responsible for the review action and review scope they actually performed, not for every unstated property of the artifact.

Approval means no more than the scope established by the review record and repository policy.

`review performed != universal validation`

## Merge and repository-control responsibility

A human or account authorized to merge into the canonical repository is responsible for that repository-scoped merge decision under the current repository policy.

That fact does not establish universal legal authority, factual truth, authorship of all merged material, or responsibility for later third-party reuse.

`repository control != universal authority`

`merge decision != truth certification`

## AI and delegated execution

AI systems, agents and automation may generate, transform, inspect or propose changes, but their participation does not silently transfer human or organizational responsibility.

Where a human explicitly authorizes a repository action, the provenance record should preserve both the tool/agent contribution where known and the human authorization event where applicable.

`AI assistance != responsibility transfer`

`delegated execution != delegated legal responsibility`

## Forks, mirrors and downstream implementations

A fork, mirror, archive or downstream implementation is responsible for its own modifications, claims, releases and operational effects.

It does not automatically inherit:

- canonical project status;
- canonical successor status;
- endorsement by the canonical repository;
- repository governance authority;
- responsibility of the original project for downstream changes.

`fork != canonical successor`

`copy != endorsement`

Downstream maintainers should preserve applicable attribution and should clearly identify modifications that are not part of the canonical repository lineage.

## Canonical successor responsibility

A successor state becomes canonical for this repository only through an explicit repository-governance event that establishes the successor relationship.

Chronology alone, a later timestamp, a higher version string, a fork with more activity, or a copied checkpoint does not establish canonical succession.

`later != canonical successor`

`popularity != canonical successor`

## Conflict and correction responsibility

When responsibility, provenance, attribution or successor status is disputed, the project should preserve the disputed historical evidence and add a correction, supersession or resolution record rather than silently rewrite history.

`correction != erasure`

`supersession != historical deletion`

## Legal and assurance boundary

This document does not itself establish legal identity, copyright ownership, legal authority, liability allocation, factual truth, causality, moral correctness, universal canonicality or PoAI/V conformance.

Those questions may require additional contracts, licenses, law, evidence or protocol layers.

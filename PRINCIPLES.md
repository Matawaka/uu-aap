# Principles of Augmented Mind for UU-AAP

These principles are normative constraints on the design of UU-AAP. A future version SHOULD NOT weaken them silently.

## P1. Human agency, not human purity

The protocol exists to preserve meaningful human agency in AI-augmented creation, not to reward the absence of AI.

Use of AI MUST NOT by itself lower a conformance level, trust label or authorship status.

## P2. Augmentation must expand choice, not conceal delegation

AI participation is compatible with human authorship when humans retain meaningful authority over relevant decisions. The protocol records **where authority was exercised**, rather than assuming that generation equals authorship.

## P3. Responsibility is scoped and distributable

No person should be forced to accept fictional responsibility for an entire socio-technical chain merely because they are the visible author.

Responsibility MUST be expressible by scope, including intent, concept selection, factual verification, editorial approval, publication, legal compliance, technical binding and independent review.

## P4. Decision provenance is more informative than token provenance

The protocol prioritizes why material alternatives were accepted, rejected or transformed. It MUST NOT reduce authorship to counting tokens, keystrokes or model-generated spans.

## P5. Uncertainty is first-class information

A transparent system MUST be able to say “unknown”, “provisional”, “disputed”, “speculative” or “not verified”.

The protocol MUST NOT force false certainty merely to produce a complete manifest.

## P6. Transparency is proportional, not total

Transparency does not mean total exposure of the creative process.

Implementations MUST support selective disclosure, redaction and private evidence. Full prompt histories, private conversations, confidential sources and biometric traces MUST NOT be mandatory for ordinary conformance.

## P7. Contestability and appeal

Any consequential provenance or review claim SHOULD be challengeable. Corrections, disputes and reviewer disagreement MUST be representable without deleting the historical record.

## P8. History is durable; judgments are revisable

Published records SHOULD be append-only or versioned. Errors MUST be correctable through successor records rather than silent rewriting.

## P9. Verification is not truth

Cryptographic integrity can show that a declaration is bound to an artifact and has not been altered. It cannot prove that the declaration is honest or that the work is factually correct.

Interfaces MUST distinguish integrity, evidence, review and truth claims.

## P10. No universal trust score

UU-AAP MUST NOT define one scalar score that collapses authorship, factuality, integrity, review quality and AI participation into a single ranking.

## P11. Interoperability before proprietary lock-in

The protocol SHOULD reuse open standards and SHOULD remain implementable by competing registries, publishers and validators.

No single vendor, publisher or model provider SHOULD be required for conformance.

## P12. The protocol itself remains corrigible

UU-AAP is subject to the same philosophy it asks of authors: its assumptions should be visible, its revisions attributable, its disagreements preserved, and its governance open to correction.
